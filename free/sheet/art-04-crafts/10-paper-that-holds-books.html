Scissors only, with a grown-up in the room. If a step seems to want a knife, snip or fold instead.
What you need: paper · tape · books · scissors · a ruler · a pencil. Three or four sheets of the same paper; two piles of books a hand-span apart, and more books to load.
ShapeHow to make it
FlatLay the sheet flat across the gap between the two piles.
A tubeRoll the sheet into a tube about as thick as your thumb and tape it along the seam. Stand it up on end.
A concertinaFold the sheet backwards and forwards like a fan, about a finger-width each fold. Lay it across the gap.
Use the same paper every time, or you only learn that thick paper is strong.

Why It Works

Making · Crafts · Page 3 of 5

DIRECTIONS: Read the box, then look at the pictures end-on.
Same paper, so why stronger? Bend something and the top squashes, the bottom stretches, and the middle does almost nothing. Strength comes from material FAR from the middle. A flat sheet is all middle.
A tube does it in every direction; a concertina in one. Push a concertina the other way and it folds like a fan.
  1. Push down on your concertina from the side instead of from above. What happens, and why?
  2. Why is a tube better than a concertina, using the word middle?

Build a Bridge

Making · Crafts · Page 5 of 5

DIRECTIONS: Span the gap between two chairs with paper and tape, and hold up as much as you can.
The rules: paper and tape only · it has to span the whole gap on its own · and there is a limit of five sheets, so you cannot win by using more paper. Weigh what it holds, or count books.
Try at least three: several thin tubes instead of one fat one · tubes taped side by side · a concertina on two tubes · a tube inside a tube.
