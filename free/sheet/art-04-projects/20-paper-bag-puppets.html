Where the Face Goes

Making · Projects · Page 2 of 6

DIRECTIONS: Read the three bands before you draw anything. A whole face on the flap splits when it opens.
Do not draw the whole face on the flap: when the jaw drops it rips down the middle. Split the face across the fold, so opening the mouth shows something hidden.
Draw in this order and it cannot go wrong.

1. Put your hand in and open the mouth. Hold it open.
2. With your other hand, draw a line right along the fold in pencil.
3. Take your hand out, lay it flat, and now you can see exactly which paper is which.
4. Eyes above the line. Inside of the mouth just below it. Everything else lower down.

The strip just below the fold is the best bit of the whole puppet, because it is the only part the audience has not seen yet. Put something worth waiting for there.
  1. What is inside your puppet’s mouth that nobody expects? Not teeth — everybody does teeth. Something that makes a person laugh the first time it opens.

A Theatre From One Box

DIRECTIONS: Cut one rectangle out of a box. Get it in the right place.
Cut the window HIGH. A deep wall of box below it hides your arms. Cut it in the middle and your arms are on stage all show.
The hole reaches down past your wrist, so it shows.
