The Two That Are Harder to Draw

American Sign Language · Page 2 of 4

DIRECTIONS: Parts four and five. Try number 4 with your own hand.
4orientation
Which way the hand faces. Palm up or palm down, fingers pointing at you or away.
Try it: hold your hand flat, palm down, then turn it over. Same shape, same place, different orientation.
5the face
What the face is doing. Not the feeling — the grammar. The eyebrows turn the same hands into a statement, or a question, or a different kind of question. Sheet 30 is about nothing else.
Swap any one part and you get a different word, not a similar one.

Why Five and Not One

American Sign Language · Page 4 of 4

DIRECTIONS: Answer in your own words.
  1. English builds a word out of one kind of part — sounds, in a row, one after another. ASL builds one out of five kinds of part, all at the same time. What can you do with five at once that you cannot do with a row?
  2. A sign has a handshape, a place, a movement, a direction it faces, and a face. Guess: is that more or less to remember than the spelling of an English word? Say why you think so.
