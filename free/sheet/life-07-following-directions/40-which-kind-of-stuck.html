DIRECTIONS: There are five kinds of stuck, each with its own first move. Which kind is each one below?
1. “I do not know what it means.” There is a word, or a whole question, that does not turn into anything when you read it.
Read it out loud, and underline the exact bit that does not make sense. That is what to ask about — usually one word, not the whole sheet.
2. “I know what it wants and I cannot start.” You understand the question. The page is still blank.
Write down what you already know — the numbers, the facts it gave you, in a list. Not the answer. Starting is a thing your hand does, and this is the smallest true thing you can write.
3. “I did it and got something odd.” You have an answer and it looks wrong, or nothing like the others.
Check the last line you wrote, not the whole thing. It is nearly always in the last line, and starting again throws away the part that was fine.
4. “I have forgotten how.” You have met this before and the method is gone.
Find one you did before — earlier on this sheet, or the sheet it came from. You are looking for the method, not for the answer to copy.
5. “I know how and I do not want to.” You could do it. There are nineteen more after it.
This one is not stuck. It is tired, and it has a different fix: do one, then stop for a bit. Pretending it is a thinking problem is how a short break turns into an hour.
