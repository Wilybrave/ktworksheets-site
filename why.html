Kitchen Table

Why worksheets

A worksheet is a hand, a pencil and a grown-up. That is the whole argument.

There is no shortage of apps that will teach a child something. This one prints paper, on purpose, and it is worth saying plainly why.

A pencil is a fine motor skill

Forming a letter is a physical act. It uses the small muscles of the hand, it needs the eye to check the hand, and it takes the kind of practice that only happens when the tool leaves a mark you can see and be dissatisfied with.

A finger on glass does none of that. It is not a worse version of holding a pencil, it is a different action entirely — and the one that school, forms, exams and a shopping list all still ask for.

Paper does not do the thinking

A sheet cannot mark itself, hint, autocomplete, or move on when the child stalls. That sounds like a shortcoming and it is the point: the pause where nothing happens is where the thinking goes.

An app fills that pause. A page leaves it open, and something has to come into it.

It is meant to be done out loud

Almost every sheet here is better with somebody sitting next to it. Not supervising — talking. "How did you get that?" is worth more than the answer, and it only happens if there is a person there to ask it.

That is where the vocabulary comes from too. A child explaining why they think the answer is nine is doing more with language in that sentence than a whole page of matching exercises asks of them.

And it is good for the grown-up

This is the part nobody puts on the box. Twenty minutes at a table with a specific, finishable task is one of the few settings where a parent and a child are working on the same thing at the same time, with something to point at.

The sheet is a reason to sit down together that neither of you has to invent. The learning is real, and so is the rest of it — and it is not obvious which one matters more.

What a worksheet is not for

It will not introduce an idea a child has never met. It will not replace being read to. It is not a curriculum, and a child who does forty of them has practiced forty things rather than learned to think.

They are practice, and practice is what makes the taught thing stick. Anything claiming more than that is selling.
