Crossing the Lines

Art · Drawing · Page 2 of 5

DIRECTIONS: Copy each patch into the empty box beside it.

Two ways to get darker. Closer lines, or a second set of lines going the other way across the first. Crossing them is called cross-hatching, and it gets very dark without the lines having to be tiny — which matters, because tiny lines are slow and a hand gets tired long before the drawing is finished.

Where Is the Light?

Art · Drawing · Page 3 of 5

DIRECTIONS: Three things happen because of where the light is. Find all three on the picture.
Where the light is decides:
1 — the highlight, the brightest spot, on the side facing the light. 2 — the dark side, opposite it. 3 — the cast shadow on the ground, stretching AWAY from the light.
Which means a drawing with the light on the right and the shadow on the right as well is not untidy. It is impossible — and it is the commonest mistake there is, because "shadows go underneath" gets learned as a fact.

1. Highlight. Leave it white. The most common shading mistake is going over the whole ball.

2. Dark side. Hatch it, getting darker as you go further from the light.

3. Cast shadow. Dark, and on the opposite side from the light. Not underneath.

Now shade this one. The light is in the same place. Use your value scale from page 1 — nearly white at the highlight, darkest at the far edge.
