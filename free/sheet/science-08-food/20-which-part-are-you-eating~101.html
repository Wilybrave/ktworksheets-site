DIRECTIONS: Root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit or seed: which part of the plant is each vegetable?
RootAnchors the plant and pulls water and minerals out of the soil. Often a store cupboard as well, which is why so many are worth eating.
StemCarries water up and food down. Holds everything else in the air.
LeafWhere food is made out of light. Thin and flat, to catch as much as possible.
FlowerThe part that makes seeds. Usually short-lived, and usually the most fussed-over part of the plant.
FruitWhatever grows around the seeds to protect them or get them carried away. Not always sweet, and not always called a fruit.
SeedA whole new plant packed up with a food supply to start it off. Which is why seeds are the richest things on the list.

The Potato Argument

What Are You Actually Eating?

DIRECTIONS: Two arguments you can settle at home.
A potato is not a root. It is a stem.

Its eyes are buds, and only stems have buds. Leave a potato in a cupboard for three weeks and shoots grow from the eyes; a carrot just goes soft.

And half the drawer is fruit

A fruit is whatever grew around the seeds, sweet or not. Tomato, cucumber, pepper, zucchini, eggplant, pumpkin, green bean: all fruit.

So what is a vegetable?

Not a plant part at all. Vegetable is a cooking word: a plant piece served with the main course. It picks up roots, flowers, fruits and seeds alike.

Try it: find all six parts in the vegetable drawer. Which kitchen food are you least sure about? How would you settle it?
