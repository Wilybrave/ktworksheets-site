Most people write this:
I get up. I get dressed. I have breakfast. I go to school.
The same morning, looked at properly. 20 steps:
  1. The alarm goes and I turn it off without opening my eyes.
  2. I lie there for a bit.
  3. I push the covers off with my feet.
  4. I sit on the edge of the bed.
  5. I find my slippers with my feet, and only one of them is there.
  6. I look under the bed for the other one.
  7. I go to the bathroom.
  8. I wash my face with cold water because it wakes me up.
  9. I clean my teeth for two minutes.
  10. I go back to my room.
  11. I take off my pajamas and put them under my pillow.
  12. I put on my socks first because the floor is cold.
  13. I put on my school sweater.
  14. I move the cat off my school bag.
  15. I check the bag has my reading book in it.
  16. I go downstairs, missing the third step because it creaks.
  17. I get a bowl out of the cupboard on the left.
  18. I pour the cereal in before the milk.
  19. I eat it standing up because I am late.
  20. I put my shoes on by the door.
The small bits make it yours: the missing sock, the creaky step, the cat.

Can You See In the Dark?

Find out for yourself with a box and a small thing to hide in it.

Name
Eyes cannot make light, only catch it. No light in the box, nothing to see.
Try this, then write what happened
  1. Put a small toy in a shoebox and put the lid on.
  2. Make a tiny hole in the lid and look through it. Can you see the toy?
  3. Now lift the lid a crack so a bit of light gets in. Look again.
  4. Now take the lid right off.
With the lid on, what could you see?
With the lid open a crack, what could you see?
So what does your eye need before it can see anything?
Say the rule: light has to get to my eye.
