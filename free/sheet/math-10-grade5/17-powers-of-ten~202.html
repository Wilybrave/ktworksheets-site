DIRECTIONS: The decimal point stays still. Multiplying by 10 slides every digit one place left; dividing slides it one place right.
Worked example — 4.7 × 100 and 4.7 ÷ 10. 100 is two tens, so the digits slide two places left: 470. Dividing by 10 slides them one place right: 0.47. Fill an empty place with a 0 so the digits stay where they slid to.
Part A — Each place is 10 times the place to its right.
  1. The 6 in 6,000 is worth times the 6 in 600.
  2. In 3.33, the 3 in the ones place is worth times the 3 in the hundredths place.
  3. The 7 in 0.07 is worth of the 7 in 0.7.
  4. In 999.9, the 9 in the hundreds place is worth times the 9 in the tenths place.
