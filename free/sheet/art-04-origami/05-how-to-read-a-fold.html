How to Read a Fold

Making · Origami · Page 1 of 3

Name Date
DIRECTIONS: Every model in this book uses these lines. Read them here first.

Valley fold. The paper comes up toward you, and the crease sinks down like a valley. Dashes.

Mountain fold. The paper goes away from you, behind, and the crease sticks up like a ridge. Dash, dot, dash.

Two more words you will meet. Turn over: flip the paper like a pancake. It makes no crease, so it has no circled number. Crease and unfold: fold, press hard, open it again. The line stays as a guide for a later fold.
  1. Seen from the side, which letter does a valley fold look like: V or Λ?
  2. Fold a strip of paper with a valley fold. Now turn it over. What kind of fold is it from this side?
  3. An easy way to make a mountain fold is to turn the paper over first. Why does that work?

Push It Flat

Making · Origami · Page 3 of 3

DIRECTIONS: Hold the square by the two ends of the mountain line and push them gently toward each other.
If every line was folded the right way, the square folds itself up flat. If it fights you, one line is folded the wrong way. Find it, fold it the other way, and try again.
  1. What shape is it now that it is flat?
  2. How many flaps can you count on the front? How many on the back?
  3. Did it fold flat the first time? If not, which line was the wrong way, and how did you find it?
  4. Open it back to a square. Folding a square in four uses two middle lines. This square has only one. Which middle line is missing?
  5. Draw what you made. Put a dot on the point that used to be the very middle of the square.
