Kitchen Table

Who makes this

One person, at a kitchen table, with two children who test everything first.

I am a developer and a dad of two. This started because I wanted decent worksheets for my own kids and kept finding the same thing everywhere: pages that look right, are quick to make, and fall apart the moment a child takes them seriously.

Yes, AI helped make these

It would be strange to pretend otherwise, and I would rather say it here than have you wonder.

What AI is good at is volume and patience — generating a hundred variations of a page, and never getting bored on the ninetieth. What it is bad at is knowing when it is wrong. It will hand you a page of sums where the answers run down the column in order, or a matching exercise solvable without reading a word, and it will look exactly as confident as a good one.

So the checking is the work

Every sheet is generated, and then every sheet is checked by a program written to disbelieve it. Answers are re-solved independently and compared. Answer columns are measured for patterns a child could ride without reading the question. Sheets are re-rendered and compared byte for byte to catch anything that quietly changed.

That found real faults — pages answerable without being read, a sudoku with two solutions, a picture that did not match its label. Those were caught by the checks, not by looking, and I would not trust a shelf this size that was only looked at.

What that means for you

I am not claiming these are perfect. I am claiming they are checked, that the checks are specific, and that when something is wrong I would like to know so it can be fixed for everybody rather than apologized for once.

Still being made

My two children are the reason this exists, and their education is the thing I am actually devoted to — the shelf is what taking that seriously turned into. They are still in school, so I am still making these. Whatever they are working on next tends to become the next thing here, which is why the shelf grows in the order it does rather than to a plan.

New sheets and new packs arrive as app updates, and so do corrections. When something is wrong — a bad answer, an instruction that reads two ways, a page that does not do what it says — it gets fixed at the source and the fix reaches everybody on the next update, not just whoever reported it.

Anything you have already printed keeps working. An update adds pages and repairs faults; it does not take the shelf you already have away from you.
