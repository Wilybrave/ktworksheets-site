Which Cat Is That?

Field Guide

DIRECTIONS: Twelve cats, all side on. Start with the first: the plain ordinary cat.
Each pair side by side differs by ONE thing.
British Shorthair
The plain one. Round head, full cheeks, short thick legs — the cat the others depart from.
Persian
The same round head, but the face is pushed FLAT and the coat is long enough to hide the legs.
Siamese
Long and slender, with a wedge-shaped head and ears far too big for it.
Devon Rex
Bigger ears still, on a smaller head — and a short curly coat.
Scottish Fold
Ears folded FORWARD and flat, so the head looks like a smooth round cap.
American Curl
Ears curled the other way — BACKWARD, arching away from the face.
Manx
No tail at all, and back legs longer than the front, so the rump sits high.
Japanese Bobtail
A short kinked tail carried like a pom-pom — a tail that is there, not one that is missing.
Munchkin
An ordinary cat on very short legs — longer than it is tall.
Maine Coon
The biggest. Tufts on the ear tips, a ruff at the neck, and a huge bushy tail.
Sphynx
No coat at all, so the skin folds into wrinkles at the forehead and shoulders.
Bengal
Rosettes — spots with a second outline round them, not plain spots.
