White light is every color at once. The apple keeps the orange, yellow, green and blue, and sends back only red. Red is all that reaches your eye.

So the color is not in the apple.

It is in the light leaving the apple. A thing's color is the one it refuses to keep.

The Bird That Takes You to the Bees

In parts of Africa there is a small brown bird called a honeyguide. It eats beeswax, which almost nothing else does. The trouble is that the wax is inside a bees' nest, and a bird that size cannot break one open.

So it fetches a person. The honeyguide flies to somebody, chatters at them until they look up, and then flies a little way and waits. If the person follows, it goes further and waits again. In this way it can lead somebody a long distance to a nest high in a tree.

The person makes smoke to quiet the bees, opens the nest and takes the honey. What is left is the wax — and that is the bird's share. Neither of them could have had anything without the other.

The strangest part is that it works both ways. In some places people have a special call they use when they want a honeyguide, and the birds have learned what it means. Nobody taught them. It is not a trick anyone trained: it is two wild animals, one of them us, who have worked out how to ask each other for help.

Crossing the Lines

Art · Drawing · Page 2 of 5

DIRECTIONS: Copy each patch into the empty box beside it.

Two ways to get darker. Closer lines, or a second set of lines going the other way across the first. Crossing them is called cross-hatching, and it gets very dark without the lines having to be tiny — which matters, because tiny lines are slow and a hand gets tired long before the drawing is finished.

Where Is the Light?

Art · Drawing · Page 3 of 5

DIRECTIONS: Three things happen because of where the light is. Find all three on the picture.
Where the light is decides:
1 — the highlight, the brightest spot, on the side facing the light. 2 — the dark side, opposite it. 3 — the cast shadow on the ground, stretching AWAY from the light.
Which means a drawing with the light on the right and the shadow on the right as well is not untidy. It is impossible — and it is the commonest mistake there is, because "shadows go underneath" gets learned as a fact.

1. Highlight. Leave it white. The most common shading mistake is going over the whole ball.

2. Dark side. Hatch it, getting darker as you go further from the light.

3. Cast shadow. Dark, and on the opposite side from the light. Not underneath.

Now shade this one. The light is in the same place. Use your value scale from page 1 — nearly white at the highlight, darkest at the far edge.
